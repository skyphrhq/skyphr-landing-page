## What does implementing AI actually involve?

Implementing AI means putting an AI system into a real business workflow so it does useful work every day, not just in a demo.

Every AI implementation has three parts:

- **The model.** The AI itself, such as a large language model (LLM), a vision model, or a prediction model.
- **Your data.** The documents, records, and history that make the AI specific to your business.
- **The workflow.** The connections to your CRM, ERP, helpdesk, website or app that let the AI take action.

Most teams spend their time choosing the model. In practice, data and workflow decide whether the project succeeds. A great model with no access to your systems is just a clever chatbot.

You'll also hear this called AI adoption, AI integration, or AI transformation. The idea is the same: making AI part of how your business runs.

## Ad hoc AI experiments vs planned AI implementation: what's the difference?

Aspect	Ad hoc AI experiments	Planned AI implementation
Starting point	A tool someone liked	A business problem with a cost
Data	Copied and pasted manually	Connected, cleaned and controlled
Success measure	"It looks impressive"	One clear, tracked metric
Risk and security	Usually ignored	Planned from day one
Result after 6 months	Abandoned or unmanaged	Used daily and improving

## How does an AI implementation work? A typical project

1. You pick one business problem that costs real time or money.
2. You check whether the data needed to solve it exists and can be accessed.
3. A small pilot is built for that one workflow, with guardrails and human review.
4. Real users test it, and results are measured against the goal you set.
5. If it works, the system is connected more deeply to your tools and rolled out.
6. Usage, cost and quality are monitored, and the system improves over time.

The questions below map to these stages. Answer them in order, and most of the common mistakes disappear.

## 20 questions to ask when implementing AI

**Strategy and goals**

1. **What exact problem are we solving?** "Use AI in support" is not a problem. "Reply to routine support enquiries within five minutes" is.
2. **How will we measure success?** Pick one primary metric, such as hours saved, response time, leads converted or errors reduced.
3. **Does this problem actually need AI?** Some tasks are solved faster and cheaper with simple automation, a better form or a cleaner process.
4. **Who owns this project on the business side?** AI projects without a clear business owner drift. The owner decides priorities and signs off on results.

**Data readiness**

5. **What data does the AI need, and where does it live today?** List the sources: documents, spreadsheets, CRM records, emails or call transcripts.
6. **Is that data accurate and up to date?** AI repeats whatever it is given. Outdated policies and duplicate records lead to confident wrong answers.
7. **Who owns and can access this data?** Check permissions early. Some data may belong to clients or partners and come with contract limits.
8. **Will our data be used to train someone else's model?** Read the provider's terms. For business data, the answer should be no.

**Technology and integration**

9. **Which systems does the AI need to connect to?** An AI that can't read your CRM or update your helpdesk creates extra copy-paste work.
10. **Should we build custom, buy off-the-shelf or combine both?** Ready-made tools suit general tasks. Core, customer-facing workflows usually need a custom fit.
11. **Which AI model fits this task, and can we switch later?** Models improve fast. A good setup lets you change models without rebuilding everything.
12. **Can this pilot scale into a full system?** Build the pilot on a foundation that can grow, so success doesn't mean starting over.

**Risk, security and compliance**

13. **What happens when the AI is wrong?** Plan for mistakes. Decide which outputs need human approval and how errors get caught and fixed.
14. **Which regulations apply to our data?** In India, the DPDP Act governs personal data. Sector rules, such as RBI guidelines for payments, may add more. Global customers may bring GDPR into scope.
15. **How do we keep sensitive data safe?** Look at encryption, role-based access, audit logs and where data is processed and stored.
16. **What are the AI's limits and rules?** Define what it may say, what it must never do and when it should hand over to a person.

**People, cost and return**

17. **How will this change our team's daily work?** Explain the change early. Teams adopt AI faster when they see it removes their most boring tasks.
18. **Who will maintain and improve the system after launch?** AI is not "set and forget". Someone needs to review outputs, update data and tune prompts.
19. **What is the full cost, not just the build?** Include model usage fees, hosting, integration, monitoring and ongoing improvement. Usage-based costs grow with volume.
20. **When do we expect to see a return?** Set a realistic review point, such as 60 or 90 days after launch, and decide in advance what "worth continuing" looks like.

## The hidden cost of skipping these questions

Try this simple calculation for your business:

> **Months spent on the wrong AI project × monthly project cost + team hours lost to rework × hourly cost = cost of a poorly planned AI project**

That number doesn't include the harder loss: a team that tried AI once, saw it fail, and now resists the next attempt. A few hours spent on these questions upfront is the cheapest insurance an AI project can have.

- **Internal knowledge:** an assistant that answers staff questions from your own documents.

## Questions to ask your AI vendor or development partner

Before you sign, ask these questions:

- **Have you built something similar before?** Ask for a live demo or a case study, not just slides.
- **How will you test accuracy before launch?** A good partner tests with your real examples and shares the results.
- **Who owns the code, prompts and data?** You should own what you pay for, including transcripts and outputs.
- **How do you handle security and compliance?** Ask where data is stored, who can access it and how it is logged.
- **What does support look like after go-live?** Clarify monitoring, fixes, updates and response times.
- **Can we start small?** A partner who pushes a large build before a pilot is a warning sign.

## Frequently asked questions

What is the first step in implementing AI in a business?
Pick one specific problem that costs your business real time or money, and decide how you'll measure success. Choosing the tool comes after that, not before.

How much does it cost to implement AI?
It depends on the scope. A focused pilot on one workflow costs far less than a company-wide platform. Plan for ongoing costs too, such as model usage, hosting, and improvements, not just the initial build.

How long does an AI implementation take?
A pilot on a single workflow can often go live in a few weeks. Larger systems with several integrations take longer, mostly depending on how ready your data is.

Do we need a lot of data to implement AI?
Not always. Many modern AI systems work well with your existing documents, FAQs, and records. What matters more is that the data is accurate, current, and accessible.

Is it better to build custom AI or buy an off-the-shelf tool?
Off-the-shelf tools are quick to start and fine for general tasks. For customer-facing or core workflows, custom AI usually fits your rules, systems, and data far better. Many businesses combine both.

How do we keep our data safe when implementing AI?
Use business-grade AI services with clear data protection terms, control access by role, and log every interaction. Keep sensitive data out of free consumer tools, and check which data protection laws apply to you.
